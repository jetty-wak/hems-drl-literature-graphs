## Why cited in this thesis

- Paper uses cost formulation C = sum(buy_price * import - sell_price * export) * dt under multiple tariff structures as primary benchmark metric. Directly parallels Ch8 Eq. kpi-cost and the thesis tariff × tier comparison design.
- Paper reports SCR as a performance metric alongside cost under different tariff structures; supports use of SCR as a complementary non-economic benchmark metric.
- Paper reports PAR or peak demand as a secondary performance metric alongside cost for PV-battery households under demand-charge tariff.
- Same justification as row 1; code implements the same formula.

## Thesis claims supported

- **[Ch2]** Hence, different tariff regimes do not merely change costs; they can also change what counts as good battery control
- **[Ch2]** For this reason, residential EMS studies are often more informative when they do not assess performance under only one tariff regime
- **[Ch2]** Optimisation baselines are useful because they show how schedules change under different pricing structures
- **[Ch2]** More recent tariff analyses show that dispatch incentives change materially once asymmetric feed-in compensation and post-net-metering settlement rules are introduced
- **[Ch2]** Because tariff structures alter the behaviour that counts as good battery control, controller comparisons should test whether conclusions remain stable when the pricing rule changes and should report economic, grid-relevant, and feasibility metrics together
- **[Ch4]** For this reason, tariff design is treated here as part of the benchmark definition
- **[Ch4]** This makes it easier to distinguish between arbitrage-oriented behaviour and peak-reduction behaviour
