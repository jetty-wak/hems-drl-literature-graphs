## Thesis claims supported

- **[Ch2]** It is to show why a credible comparison between optimisation and DRL should make boundary assumptions, information sets, constraint handling, tariff assumptions, and KPI definitions explicit before performance differences are interpreted
- **[Ch2]** Reported DRL performance is highly sensitive to how the reward function combines cost, comfort, and battery-use terms
- **[Ch3]** Demand-side management (DSM) is the wider consumer-side umbrella
- **[Ch3]** This makes the Markov decision process (MDP) a useful abstraction: it turns HEMS control into a repeated decision problem in which the controller must learn how present actions affect future costs, state of charge, comfort, and feasibility
- **[Ch3]** The reward is anchored in negative operating cost, with additional penalty terms used only when a labelled variant introduces peak, comfort, degradation, or task-completion objectives
