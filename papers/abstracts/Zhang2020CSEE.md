# Deep reinforcement learning for energy system optimization

**BibTeX key:** `Zhang2020CSEE`  
**Cited in:** Ch2, Ch3/4

## Claims supported in thesis

- It is to show why a credible comparison between optimisation and DRL should make boundary assumptions, information sets, constraint handling, tariff assumptions, and KPI definitions explicit before performance differences are interpreted
- Reported DRL performance is highly sensitive to how the reward function combines cost, comfort, and battery-use terms
- Demand-side management (DSM) is the wider consumer-side umbrella
- This makes the Markov decision process (MDP) a useful abstraction: it turns HEMS control into a repeated decision problem in which the controller must learn how present actions affect future costs, state of charge, comfort, and feasibility
- The reward is anchored in negative operating cost, with additional penalty terms used only when a labelled variant introduces peak, comfort, degradation, or task-completion objectives
