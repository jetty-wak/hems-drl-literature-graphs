## Claims supported in thesis

- Second, accurate problem formulation requires domain expertise to encode device dynamics, cost structures, and constraint logic, creating a model-specification barrier that grows with system complexity
- Reported outcomes also depend on design choices such as reward specification, hyperparameter settings, scenario construction, and the information available during training and evaluation
- Comparative results are easier to interpret when information sets, optimisation horizons, and training budgets are reported explicitly
- Sample inefficiency and training cost remain practical limitations in DRL energy-management studies, especially when comparisons against optimisation baselines do not report training budgets explicitly
